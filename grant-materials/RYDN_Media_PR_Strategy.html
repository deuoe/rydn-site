Media & PR Strategy

How RYDN gets into the press as a young Canadian nonprofit — concrete tactics, pitch templates, and award shortlists.

1 Local-first, always

Don't pitch the New York Times before you've been in your community paper. The press pyramid below moves from easiest to hardest. Build credibility tier by tier — each rung gives you a quotable mention that makes the next pitch easier.

Tier Examples Difficulty What works
🟢Free community calendars Richmond Hill Liberal, North York Mirror, BlogTO events Easy List workshops, milestones
🟢Community / ethnic press Shahrvand, Salam Toronto, Sing Tao, ethnic radio Easy Iranian-Canadian angle is rare and compelling
🟡Education-focused outlets University Affairs, Maclean's Education, EdCan Network Medium Pitch trend stories + data
🟡Local TV / radio CBC Metro Morning, CityTV, CP24 Medium Short interviews on Education Day-type segments
🔴National papers Globe & Mail, Toronto Star, National Post Hard Need a strong hook plus impact numbers

2 What makes RYDN newsworthy right now

You have five distinct story angles already. Any one of them is enough for an editor to bite. Match the angle to the journalist's beat.

🇮🇷→🇨🇦 Founder angle. Iranian immigrant founds Canadian nonprofit for newcomer students. Ethnic press loves this. So does CBC Local.
🤖 AI for education. Multilingual AI matchmaker for free youth advising. Tech and education reporters love this.
🇨🇦 Free 1-on-1 for newcomers. Students 2–3 years ahead advising those behind them. Community and education angle.
🌐 Multilingual mentorship. Persian, Hebrew, Spanish, French support. Diversity and inclusion stories.
🎓 UN SDG alignment. Canadian nonprofit publicly committing to four UN goals. Tied to UN news cycles.

3 Concrete next steps

Week 1 — Set up infrastructure
  • Press page live at rydn.ca/press ✅
  • Build a list of 5–10 specific journalists who cover education / newcomers / nonprofits in Canada.
  • Sign up for HARO (helpareporter.com) — free. Journalists post requests for sources; you reply.
  • Optional: sign up for Cision or Muck Rack if budget allows (paid).
Week 2 — First pitches
  • Send personalized email to each journalist with a 1-line description, a specific story angle, a founder quote, and a link to the press page.
  • Submit RYDN to local community calendars (Richmond Hill Public Library, Toronto Star community board, BlogTO, Toronto.com).
Week 3 — Earned mentions
  • Pitch a guest op-ed to Maclean's Education, Toronto Star, or Globe Education. Title: "What I learned founding a free youth advising platform as an Iranian-Canadian immigrant."
  • Reach out to podcast hosts in the education and immigrant entrepreneur space.
Month 2+ — Authority building
  • Awards: Apply to Top 25 Canadian Immigrants, YMCA Peace Medal, 30 Under 30 lists, Canadian Charity Awards. Even nominations create press hooks.
  • Speaking: Pitch to Educause, TEDx Richmond Hill, school board events.
  • Partnerships: Co-announce wins with TDSB / YRDSB.

4 Founder pitch template

Use this verbatim. Replace bracketed bits with your specifics. Personalize the opening with a specific reference to a story the journalist recently wrote — that one sentence doubles your reply rate.

Subject: Story idea — Iranian-Canadian immigrant founds free youth advising platform aligned with UN SDGs Hi [Journalist's first name], I've been following your work on [specific recent piece they wrote]. I'm Sam, co-founder of RYDN — a Canadian nonprofit that connects students with free 1-on-1 advisors who are only 2–3 years ahead of them academically. I founded RYDN after my own journey from the University of Guilan in Iran to George Brown to York University, navigating how confusing the Canadian education system is for newcomer families. We now have 14 student advisors helping high school students across the GTA — for free, in 5 languages including Persian and Hebrew. Our work is publicly aligned with 4 UN Sustainable Development Goals. I'd love to talk to you about [specific angle relevant to their beat]. Could we set up a 15-minute call? Press kit, founder bios, and quotes ready to use: rydn.ca/press Cheers, Sam Sina Ansari Movahed Co-founder, RYDN (647) 498-3938
Pro tip: Send between Tuesday and Thursday, 9–11 AM Eastern. Journalists open and reply at higher rates in that window. Avoid Mondays (inbox cleanup) and Fridays (winding down).

6 Awards to apply to

Each gives you a press release angle even if you don't win — being a finalist or nominee is itself newsworthy. Build a calendar of these so you never miss a deadline.

  • Top 25 Canadian Immigrants — annual, March deadline. Strong fit for Sam's founder story.
  • YMCA Peace Medal — community impact focus.
  • Charity Village Awards — Canadian nonprofit sector awards.
  • Imagine Canada Standards — accreditation, not an award. Huge credibility signal once obtained.
  • Ontario Trillium Foundation grants — receiving one comes with press support.
  • Lieutenant Governor's Community Volunteer Award (Ontario).
  • The Globe and Mail Best Workplaces — once RYDN has paid staff.
  • RBC Top 25 Canadian Immigrant Awards — corporate-backed, strong PR.
  • The Indus Entrepreneurs (TiE) Toronto — South Asian network, often open to Iranian-Canadians too.
Pro tip: Apply to 3–5 awards per year. Even being a finalist gives you a quotable line for grant applications: "Finalist, 2026 Top 25 Canadian Immigrant Awards."
